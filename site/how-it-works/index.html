How Plainly decides

An AI model reads the letter and, later, explains it. It does not decide the verdict. Fixed rules written in plain code do that, using quotes that must appear in an independent reading of the letter.

The steps

  1. Your device shrinks the photo to at most 1.5 MB. A PDF's first three pages are drawn into one image, also on your device.
  2. Two readers, independently. Amazon Textract reads every line of text. Separately, Amazon Nova notes who the letter claims to be from, its date, deadlines, payment requests and threats, each with the exact words it found them in.
  3. Plain code checks. Each quote from Nova must appear in Textract's reading, or it is marked unconfirmed and counts for less. Phone numbers, web addresses and emails are pulled out by pattern matching, not by the model, and compared with an official contacts list. Deadlines like "within 21 days of this notice" are worked out by arithmetic.
  4. The verdict and the receipts. The rules give a score, the score gives the verdict, and every rule leaves a line in the receipts, whether it passed or not.
  5. Amazon Nova explains. A second request writes the explanation, actions and a reply draft in your language. It is given the checked facts and dates; it does not work out dates itself.

The rules

Each rule is a few lines of plain code that run on the letter's text and on the quotes the model found. A strong sign adds 3 points, a warning sign adds 1, a note adds nothing.

The verdict

  • Likely scam when the score is 3 or more: one strong sign, or three warning signs.
  • Consistent with a genuine letter only when all of these hold: the organisation is on our official list, at least one of its web addresses or phone numbers in the letter matches the list, the score is 0, and the quotes were confirmed by the independent reading.
  • Can't tell in every other case.

Quote checking. A quote counts as confirmed when, ignoring case, spacing and punctuation, it appears in Textract's reading of the letter or matches a passage very closely (a similarity of 0.85 or more). An unconfirmed strong sign is lowered to a warning sign and labelled as unconfirmed.

The official contacts list. Each organisation has its official web domains and phone numbers, the page we took them from and the date we checked. Web domains are matched first, phone numbers second, because real organisations publish many numbers.

Why it never says "safe"

A scammer can copy a real letter exactly, down to the layout and the wording. A photo cannot prove who sent it. So the best Plainly can honestly say about a letter is that it is consistent with a genuine one.

That is why every result, including the reassuring ones, ends with the official number and where we found it. The last step is always a call you place yourself, to a number you did not get from the letter.

When the evidence is thin, Plainly says "Can't tell" rather than guessing. A wrong "this is fine" costs far more than an extra phone call.

Privacy

  • Your letter is processed in memory and discarded when the check ends. It is not saved to a database, a file or a log.
  • Logs hold only a request id, the verdict, which rules fired, timings and token counts. Never the words of the letter.
  • There is no share link and no account, so there is nothing about your letter to look up later.
  • To stop abuse, we count requests per network address for an hour, and total checks per day. Those counters expire on their own.
  • Your language and reading-level choice are remembered in your own browser only.

Limits

  • A short official list. We cover a small set of organisations in India, the United States and the United Kingdom. For anyone else, the best answer may be "Can't tell".
  • Scripts other than Latin. Textract does not read Devanagari. Letters in Hindi are read by the model alone and their quotes are marked as not independently confirmed.
  • Blurry or partial photos lose words, and a missed word can hide a warning sign. A clear, flat, well-lit photo of the whole page works best.
  • Scams change. The rules catch the common patterns, not every new one. A clean result is not a guarantee.
  • Not legal, tax or financial advice. For anything with money or legal steps at stake, use the official number or a trusted adviser.
