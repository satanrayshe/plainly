You get one of three answers

  1. Likely scam

    Strong warning signs, such as a demand for gift cards or a threat of arrest. Don't pay, and don't call the number in the letter.

  2. Consistent with a genuine letter. Confirm on the official number.

    Its contacts match the real organisation and nothing looks wrong. Still call the official number we show you before you act.

  3. Can't tell

    Not enough to decide either way. We show you how to check it yourself.

Plainly never says a letter is "safe". A scam can copy a real letter perfectly, so the last step is always the official number. Why we do it this way

How it decides

Your phone reads the letter. Plain code makes the call, and every check leaves a receipt you can open. People wrote the explanations.

  1. Your phone reads the letter

    Tesseract, running in your browser, turns the photo into text. The photo never leaves your device, and you can fix a misread word before anything is checked.

  2. Code checks it against rules and an official contacts list

    Plainly's rules engine on AWS Lambda runs over 20 rules: payment by gift card or crypto, threats of arrest, requests for an OTP or PIN, look-alike and short links, and more. Phone numbers and websites are compared with a list of official contacts, each with its source.

  3. Explained in words people wrote

    Explanations written by people in English, हिन्दी and Español, not generated. The real deadline as a calendar file, and a reply you can send when the letter looks genuine.

Your letter stays yours

  • Your phone or computer reads the photo. The photo is never uploaded, to Plainly or anyone else.
  • Only the words, which you can check and correct first, go to Plainly's checker. They are checked in memory and not stored.
  • Our logs record which checks ran and how long they took. Never the words of your letter.
  • No account, no email address, no tracking cookies.
  • Tip: cover full ID numbers, such as Aadhaar, Social Security or bank card numbers, before you take the photo. Plainly does not need them.
